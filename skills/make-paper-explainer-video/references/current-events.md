# Current-event research and evidence

Use this reference for company, founder, crypto, technology, legal, security, shutdown, launch, and other time-sensitive stories.

## Select a story

Prefer a just-happened event with at least one of:

- a clear rise-and-fall or invention-versus-inventor reversal;
- a surprising identity conflict, such as the attacker becoming the investigator;
- a concrete operational, legal, or money consequence;
- two or three verified numbers that make the change visible;
- primary-source pages that can carry most of the video.

Reject topics that only support generic commentary, predictions, or decorative visuals.

## Source hierarchy

Use the strongest available combination:

1. The company's or person's dated original statement.
2. Regulator, court, law-enforcement, exchange, or incident record.
3. Historical launch page, product documentation, or archived first-party account.
4. A reliable wire service or established newsroom for independent context.
5. Specialist reporting only when it supplies a clearly attributed datum unavailable above.

Use at least two source types for the central claim. Treat search snippets and social reposts as discovery aids, not final evidence.

## Claim boundaries

Record every volatile claim with its source and fact-check date. Keep these distinctions explicit:

- announced closure versus already closed;
- allegation versus admission, ruling, or verified finding;
- preliminary incident assessment versus completed forensics;
- attributed estimate versus audited result;
- chronological sequence versus proven causation;
- no evidence found versus proof that nothing happened.

If no source establishes a single cause, say so. Do not turn plausible factors into a confirmed explanation.

## Visual evidence

Build shots from real pages:

- current announcement or wire headline;
- dated historical product announcement;
- regulator or court record;
- original chart, table, incident timeline, or quoted metric;
- the paragraph that states the limitation or unknown.

Crop to legible evidence and add restrained labels, arrows, dates, and comparisons. Avoid fake people, AI-generated offices, stock trading screens, glowing dashboards, and decorative hacker imagery.

## Final checks

- Confirm names, dates, status, amounts, and present-tense wording again immediately before export.
- Put the fact-check date in the source footer and publishing copy.
- Keep unresolved causes and ongoing investigations unresolved.
- Update or withdraw the publishing package if a material fact changes before posting.
