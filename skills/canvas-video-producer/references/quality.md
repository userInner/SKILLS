# Video quality and salvage

## Acceptance rubric

Review each result on five axes:

1. Identity: face, costume, weapon, and scale remain stable.
2. Action: start, motion, consequence, and endpoint are readable.
3. Space: screen direction, gaze, target, and camera axis remain coherent.
4. Motion: subject articulation exists; the result is not only a camera pan.
5. Edit value: the clip contains usable handles and at least one decisive event.

Reject or salvage a clip that passes technical validation but fails the story.

## Contact-sheet review

Create a one-second contact sheet and inspect:

- repeated poses;
- soft morphs where hard action was requested;
- return to an earlier state after the climax;
- unmotivated direction changes;
- missing endpoint;
- text, logos, duplicated subjects, or anatomy drift.

Then watch at normal speed with and without audio.

## Salvage before regeneration

Use editing when the motion is usable but pacing is weak:

- remove dead opening and tail frames;
- speed-ramp approach and follow-through;
- split one continuous clip into wide, close, and detail crops;
- hold 1–3 frames on the impact;
- add motivated white flash, shake, debris, and low-frequency impact;
- replace model music and synchronize the strongest events in editing.

Do not use effects to hide an incoherent action or identity failure.

## Technical delivery

Prefer an H.264/AAC compatibility copy for ordinary editors:

- `yuv420p`;
- square pixels;
- explicit BT.709 limited-range metadata;
- safe audio peak below -1 dB;
- complete decode without errors.

Preserve the original model output alongside the compatibility copy.
